01 · Wood 木 · 生

Spark

The day grows quiet. The mind stays awake.

The night before an important pitch, I was so nervous that I couldn’t fall asleep. The longer I stayed awake, the more anxious I became, and the harder sleep felt. I didn’t sleep at all that night.

I often overthink before bed, and I scroll my phone for too long, which only makes me more awake. I wanted to solve this, for myself and for people like me.

02 · Fire 火 · 长

Expand

I looked at what keeps people up: stimulation from one more video, unfinished thoughts about tomorrow, and the wish to reclaim time that finally feels like their own. 30.5% of U.S. adults sleep under seven hours, and more than one in four college students experience insomnia. Existing sleep products manage the environment and the body, and emotional AI manages conversation, but few systems bridge emotional offloading with a deliberate transition into silence.

I reviewed research on insomnia and pre-sleep arousal, and how light, sound, temperature, and touch affect sleep.

I ran 8 semi-structured interviews. Abigail, 20, stays up scrolling because night is the only time that feels like hers. Ryan, 22, can’t switch off as unfinished tasks come back the moment everything gets quiet. Both showed how hard it is to let the day go.

03 · Earth 土 · 化

Turn

How might we create a comforting, screen-free bedtime experience that helps young adults release the weight of the day and gently ease into rest?

The answer was not another app, but a companion to hold. I translated each sleep barrier into a design need, and each need into a feature: conversation for unfinished thoughts, guided breathing for racing minds, warmth and gentle rhythm for physical tension, and voice interaction to replace the phone.

04 · Metal 金 · 收

Refine

How Nubi works

Inside a soft silicone shell, Nubi combines an AI voice module, a breath and sleep radar, LED lights, an aroma pad, a heating module, and a vibration motor. The AI conversation moves through five stages: arrive, release, hold, settle, and fade. It listens first, then helps the user set worries aside, and gradually goes quiet.

Hardware and interface

I wired the prototype on Arduino. Breathing light patterns use color to signal what the pillow is doing, the heater turns itself off on a timer, and the radar stops everything once the user falls asleep. The companion app uses dark backgrounds, soft gradients, minimal text, and slow movement, so users can choose an activity and put the phone down.

Testing and iteration

Testing surfaced problems I fixed along the way: the lights were too bright for bedtime, the heater warmed too slowly, voice commands were sometimes missed, and the vibration motor was noisy. I then tested the wind-down sequence with 10 users. 6 of them reported using their phones less before sleep or settling down faster, and their feedback led to three additions: Nubi now stops when it detects sleep, plays white noise or the user’s own audio, and lets users customize the AI’s voice.

05 · Water 水 · 藏

Hold

People don’t always use a product the way I expect.

Testing taught me that every feature needs a simple, direct way in. If someone has to think about how to start, the calm moment is already gone.

Testers also raised concerns about the safety of the heating element and electronics. Before any overnight use, Nubi needs more safety and durability testing, and a protective layer over the warm pads. Next, I want to explore smart-home integration, so the pillow can dim the lights or adjust the room without anyone reaching for a phone.
